# Problem statement

Small analytics teams struggle to turn business requests into actionable work because requirements, ownership, and business definitions are scattered across chat and documents. Requesters cannot easily see progress, analysts repeatedly ask for missing context, and team leads lack a consistent view of waiting time and ownership.

## Who experiences it

- Business requesters need a clear way to submit and track a question.
- Analysts need exact reporting periods, datasets, definitions, and expected outputs before starting.
- Analytics leads need to know which requests are blocked, unassigned, or aging.

## Scenario

A manager asks in Discord, "Why did sales drop last week? We need a regional and product breakdown before Monday's leadership meeting."

The request does not define "last week," the comparison period, sales after or before refunds, a source dataset, a responsible analyst, or a concrete deadline. Those details become another conversation that the next analyst may need to reconstruct.

Loom imports the ask and its source link, identifies missing context, and lets the team clarify it. A reviewed proposal can update the request; an owner assigns a teammate and deadline. Approved metric definitions become reusable knowledge. Exasol records the work and computes workflow insights.

## Intended value and evaluation

The intended benefit is less repeated clarification and clearer ownership. Compare matched synthetic request batches using ordinary chat, Loom's manual workflow, and Loom with its assistant. Measure clarification rounds, time until Ready/assigned, reviewer effort, incorrect changes, and repeated questions. Measure fulfillment time separately.

No time-saving percentage, customer traction, or revenue result is claimed. Loom does not perform the underlying sales analysis, create analyst capacity, or establish the cause of a business decline.
